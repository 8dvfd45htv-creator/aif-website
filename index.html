I swam for six years and got worse the whole time.

I was not lazy. I just never wanted it. This site is what I found when I tried to work out why, by surveying my year level, keeping a diary, interviewing teachers and reading the research.

Learning output: this website, for Year 11 and 12 students.

Learning goal: To understand what keeps teenagers motivated across different areas of life, explore the science and psychology behind drive and burnout, and explore why the same motivational method can push one person forward while breaking another.

What this site is about

Most motivation advice tells you to set goals and wake up earlier. When I surveyed 19 students in my year, they said they were already reasonably motivated on a normal day, averaging 6.79 out of 10. Starting was not their problem.

What they described instead was motivation that worked for a while and then turned on them. So this site is not about how to get more motivated. It is about why motivation collapses, and why the same method can push one person forward and break another.

I am not recommending pressure, fear or competition as tools. The evidence here shows that they work for some people and do real damage to others.

Four things I found

  1. The type of motivation matters more than the amount.

    Self-determination theory says motivation comes in different kinds. Being pushed from outside and wanting something for yourself can look identical from the outside, and they end very differently. Read the theory.

  2. My peers are more sure about the harm of pressure than the benefit.

    They rated pressure's effectiveness 3.42 out of 5 and rated "the same method can destroy someone" 4.58. I started the project assuming the opposite. See the numbers.

  3. People describe their motivation one way and admit another.

    Nobody picked "proving someone wrong" as what keeps them going, but 17 of 19 said it had motivated them when asked directly. See the gap.

  4. Rewards wear out and wanting can outlive liking.

    Brain research on dopamine gives two explanations for why a reward stops working and why people keep chasing things they no longer enjoy. Read the brain section.
